Using Daily View

How families manage Daily View remotely

Daily View is built so that the people who organise your parent’s day don’t need to be in the room to do it. Here’s how it works, and how family and carers can share the job.

By Phil Martin, founder of Daily View · 6 minute read

In short

  • The screen at home simply shows the day — there’s nothing for your parent to press or set up.
  • Family and carers make changes from their own phone, tablet or computer.
  • Changes appear on the screen within about a minute.
  • You can invite others to help, and choose what each person can do.

Two parts: the screen and the dashboard

The viewer screen

A tablet in your parent’s home, kept plugged in, showing the day, date, time, today’s events and what’s next. It keeps itself up to date.

The dashboard

Where family and carers make changes. Sign in from anywhere, on any phone, tablet or computer with a web browser.

Setting up the screen

You only need to be with the tablet once, to connect it to your account.

  1. Choose a tablet — an old one you already have is usually fine. See Choosing a tablet for Daily View.
  2. On the dashboard’s Devices page, add the screen. You’ll be given a short pairing code to connect the tablet — Setting up the Daily View screen walks you through it step by step.
  3. Once it’s paired, the screen can be left alone. Everything else is done from the dashboard.

Adding what’s happening

On the Calendar page, add the things that make up your parent’s day: visits, appointments, outings and phone calls. Give each one a time and a short, plain title — written the way your parent would say it, such as “Sarah visiting” or “Hair appointment”.

Regular events

Events can repeat every week, every 2 weeks, every 4 weeks, every month or every year — ideal for a weekly lunch club, a monthly check-up or a birthday. You only need to set them up once.

Advance notice

Some events are worth mentioning before the day. For each event you can choose to show it as coming up from the day before, a few days before, or a week before — or not in advance at all.

If you don’t choose, Daily View uses sensible defaults: monthly events are shown the day before, yearly events a few days before (you can change this in Settings), and weekly events aren’t announced in advance, as they’d be on the screen almost every day.

When plans change

Edit an event and the screen will update. If something isn’t happening any more, you can cancel it, which takes it off the screen, or delete it altogether.

Keeping something off the screen

Not everything needs to be on display. An event’s visibility setting lets you keep it on the family calendar without showing it on the screen — useful for a surprise, or for something that might cause worry if seen too early.

Sending a message

From the Messages page you can put a short, important update on the screen so it stands out — for example, “Sarah’s train is late. She’ll be with you at 3pm.” You choose when a message starts and stops showing, so it doesn’t linger once it’s out of date.

Inviting family and carers

Keeping the day up to date is easier when the job is shared. From the People page, invite someone by email and choose what they can do:

  • Owner Full control: events, messages, the screen, people and settings.
  • Carer / Family member Can add and change events and messages.
  • Viewer Can see the schedule, but can’t change anything.
  • Device manager Looks after the screen and its display settings only — handy for the family member who’s good with technology.

The person you invite receives an email with a link to accept and set up their own sign-in.

Checking the screen is working

  • The Today page shows a preview of what’s on your parent’s screen.
  • The Devices page shows whether the screen is online, and lets you refresh it remotely if something doesn’t look right.

What if the internet drops?

The screen checks for updates about once a minute. If the Wi‑Fi goes down, it carries on showing the most recent update and the clock keeps running. After a few minutes, a small notice appears to show it’s offline. When the connection comes back, it catches up by itself — there’s nothing anyone needs to do.

Tips for sharing the job

  • Agree who’s in charge. One owner, and one person who looks over the week ahead — perhaps every Sunday evening.
  • Keep titles short and familiar. “Doctor” can be clearer than “GP review – Dr Patel”.
  • Less is more. A few important events are easier to take in than a long list.
  • Tell visiting carers about the screen, so they can point to it too.

Privacy

Only people you invite can see or change your parent’s schedule. You can read more in our privacy policy.
