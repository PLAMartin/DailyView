Choosing a display

Wall calendar, smart display or day calendar?

If you’re looking for something to help a parent keep track of the day, there are three common options. Each has real strengths. This guide walks through them honestly, so you can choose what suits your family.

By Phil Martin, founder of Daily View · 6 minute read

In short

  • Wall calendars are cheap and familiar, but someone needs to be there in person to keep them up to date.
  • Smart displays can be updated from anywhere, but they do a lot of other things too, which can crowd out the day’s plan.
  • Day calendar clocks show the day and date very clearly, but can’t tell your parent what’s happening.
  • The right choice depends on which questions your parent asks most — and many families use more than one.

First: what does your parent most need to know?

Before comparing products, it helps to think about the questions you hear most often.

  • If it’s mostly “What day is it?”, a clear day and date may be enough.
  • If it’s “What’s happening today?” or “When are you coming?”, they’ll need to see today’s plans as well.
  • If you live some distance away, think about who will keep it up to date, and how.

Wall calendars and whiteboards

The classic option: a paper calendar on the wall, a whiteboard in the kitchen, or a notepad beside the chair.

Good for

  • Costing very little
  • No technology, Wi‑Fi or charging
  • Being familiar and easy to write on

Watch out for

  • Someone needs to be there to update it
  • A month-view grid is busy, and it can be hard to tell which day is today
  • Old notes can linger after plans change

Best for: families who live with their parent or visit every day, and are happy to keep it updated by hand.

Smart displays

Devices such as the Amazon Echo Show or Google Nest Hub combine a screen, a speaker and a voice assistant. They can show a clock, photos and a shared calendar, and many families like them for video calls.

Good for

  • Updating a shared calendar from anywhere
  • Video calls and photos
  • Spoken reminders

Watch out for

  • They’re built to do many things — news, suggestions and rotating content can push today’s plan out of view
  • Calendar text is often small, or only shown briefly
  • Voice assistants can be confusing, or set off by accident
  • Menus and features can change with software updates

Best for: families who want video calling, and have someone comfortable setting the device up and simplifying it.

Day calendar clocks

Sometimes called dementia clocks or day clocks, these are digital displays that show the day, date and time in large letters — often with the part of the day, such as “Morning” or “Afternoon”.

Good for

  • Being very clear and easy to read
  • Nothing to learn — just plug it in
  • No Wi‑Fi needed
  • Answering “What day is it?” and “Is it morning or evening?”

Watch out for

  • They usually can’t show appointments, visits or messages
  • Where reminders are offered, they’re set on the clock itself
  • Nothing can be changed from a distance

Best for: someone whose main worry is losing track of the day or time, rather than what’s planned.
